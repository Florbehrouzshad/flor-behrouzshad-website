Market Analysis · Fall 2026

GTA Real Estate Market Outlook

Rates, Economy, Policy, Supply & Market Conditions

By Flor Behrouzshad · September 2026

The GTA real estate market is entering fall 2026 at an interesting intersection. Prices have adjusted, sales remain relatively subdued, financing conditions continue to matter, and uncertainty around trade and the broader economy remains significant. At the same time, a sharp slowdown in new housing construction raises a different question: while demand may be restrained today, what could reduced supply mean for the market in the years ahead?

Understanding the current market therefore requires looking beyond sales and prices alone.

Where the GTA Market Stands

The latest complete TRREB data, for August 2026, recorded 5,057 home sales, down 2.1% from August 2025. New listings fell more sharply to 12,075, down 14.1% year over year. The MLS® HPI Composite benchmark was 4.5% below a year earlier, while the average selling price was $993,410, down 2.7%.

Month over month, however, the seasonally adjusted benchmark was essentially flat and the average selling price edged higher. That combination matters: year-over-year prices remain lower, but the flow of new supply has also tightened.

Interest Rates and Financing

On September 2, 2026, the Bank of Canada held its policy rate at 2.25%. The policy rate is important, particularly for variable-rate borrowing, but it is not the only force affecting mortgage costs. Fixed mortgage rates are influenced more directly by bond-market yields, which can move as expectations for inflation, growth and risk change.

For real estate decisions, the practical question is therefore not simply whether the Bank of Canada changes its rate. Affordability depends on the mortgage product, qualification, household cash flow and the financing conditions available when a transaction is being considered.

Trade, Energy and Global Uncertainty

Canada’s economic outlook is also being shaped by trade uncertainty, energy prices and geopolitical developments. These factors can affect inflation, business investment, employment expectations and borrowing costs. They also influence consumer confidence — an important variable in housing demand.

This makes the current environment less suitable for decisions based on a single forecast. Buyers, sellers and investors need to consider how resilient their own plan is under more than one economic scenario.

The Supply Question

CMHC’s Fall 2026 Housing Supply Report highlights a longer-term issue. In Toronto, population-adjusted housing starts in the first half of 2026 were among the lowest levels seen in decades, and condominium project launches have largely stalled. CMHC estimates that Toronto still needs a substantially higher pace of annual housing starts over the next decade to restore affordability toward 2019 levels.

The timing is important. A weak construction pipeline does not necessarily create an immediate shortage in the resale market. Housing takes years to plan and build. The effect of today’s reduced starts can therefore emerge later, when demand conditions may be different.

Market Perspective

The GTA market is not currently giving one simple signal. Softer year-over-year prices can create opportunities for some purchasers, while the decline in new listings can alter negotiating conditions in particular neighbourhoods or property segments. At the same time, financing and economic uncertainty remain important constraints.

The useful question is not simply whether the market is “up” or “down.” It is how the specific property, location, financing structure and time horizon fit the client’s objective.

For buyers, sellers and investors, decisions should therefore be made at the intersection of current data and individual circumstances — not from headlines alone.
